- Grant: an internal grant seeded from CRIS is authoritatively sourced by its CRIS record (`proposal_number` or `award_number`) already on it, which is as good as it gets, so it counts on its own with the `url` left empty. Attach a public page as the `url` when one precisely names the grant: its NIH RePORTER project page for an NIH grant (found by grant number), or a funder award page or ClinicalTrials.gov record for a trial grant. For a grant found only via ORCID (not in CRIS), the per-grant URL its funding carries is the source; CRIS outranks it. A bare ORCID or CV profile, or an aggregator (Dimensions, Scopus), names the person or an index rather than the grant, so it is the least preferred, a last resort when nothing better is available and the page explicitly names the grant.
- Paper-derived finding (a publication, or an impact, guideline, or policy grounded in a paper): the paper's DOI (`https://doi.org/<doi>`), which the publication list carries (from OpenAlex, PubMed, ORCID); if the paper genuinely has no DOI, its PubMed record is the fallback. An index or aggregator link (Scopus, Dimensions), a publisher landing page, or a search-result URL is the least preferred, better than nothing when no DOI or PubMed record can be found.
- Trial: its ClinicalTrials.gov record.
- Profile or media: the scholar's own profile or genuine coverage; a third-party provider-rating or clinician-directory site is weaker and less impactful, so prefer the scholar's own page or real coverage when it can be found.
